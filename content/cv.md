## Research

**Coolness and Distinction: A24 and American Indie Film Since 2010**, *2025 – 2026*
Graduate thesis
- Research questions: How did A24 achieve significant economic success and cultural influence? What does this success reveal about broader changes in the indie film industry and culture? Why does a "cool" brand make sense?
- Methodology: Following Jerome Christensen on corporate authors, A24 is treated as an author with intentions and substantial control over its products. The thesis first positions A24 within the media industry and traces the relationship between its success and industrial change; it then draws on Bourdieu's theories of taste and distinction, together with brand economics, to analyze the history of cool culture and A24's brand strategy; finally it compares A24 with Miramax and Neon to analyze changes in indie film culture and A24's potential crisis.
- Conclusion: A24's success stems in large part from its use of emerging networked media, taste culture and brand strategy in response to a developing youth culture led by the new generation. In the process the company has reshaped the appeal of American independent cinema among younger audiences, generating a new form of cinephilia marked by the pursuit of "cool," loyalty to the company's brand, and the affirmation of one's own identity — rather than a primary appreciation of directors' aesthetics.

**Recurrent Mother-Daughter Conflicts: Archetypal Narratives, Immigrant Subjectivities and Ideological Mechanisms in Chinese American Cinema**, *2024*
Independent research project, Critical Media Theory course
- Research question: In Chinese American cinema, why has the same narrative pattern of mother–daughter conflict been reproduced by different filmmakers across different historical periods?
- Methodology: Following Northrop Frye, the paper traces the recurring narrative back to the myth of Demeter and Persephone and reads its recurrence as a manifestation of the collective unconscious — an unconscious that includes the "fantasy of symbiosis" proposed by psychoanalyst Iki Freud. The pattern is then examined through three lenses: the immigrant experience, commerce, and ideology.
- Conclusion: The recurring mother–daughter relationship symbolically reflects the breakdown of immigrants' "symbiotic fantasy" with their homeland and the identity anxieties it produces. Driven by the pursuit of commercial success, it is also bound up with longstanding representations of Asian women in American cinema and with audiences' Orientalist imaginary. The paper further proposes that Asian American cinema has grown increasingly privatized, detaching its protagonists from the broader social landscape and confining their stories to the family — a tendency that obscures the real social conditions of Asian Americans and turns Asian American identity into a form of generic spectacle.

**Chuzou (Asian Women in Departure): Female Identity and Cultural Negotiation in Wayne Wang's Films**, *2022*
Undergraduate thesis
- Research questions: What characterizes the representation of Asian women in Wayne Wang's films, and why does it matter culturally and film-historically?
- Methodology: Uses "chuzou" — an important topic in Chinese literary and art criticism — as a central analytical framework. Beyond physical departure, *chuzou* represents the power of women who resist oppression. Postcolonial perspectives are used to analyze this resilience within broader ideological and historical contexts.
- Conclusion: Two generations of Asian American women in Wang's films depart from their homes and established social environments in pursuit of their own identities and subjectivities. Through narratives of intergenerational conflict, ruptured intimate relationships, and reconciliation, these films represent the social dilemmas of these women and the processes of cultural negotiation. In contrast to the evil and hypersexualized "Dragon Lady" stereotype prevalent in earlier American cinema, these characters open up new possibilities for representation and narration in a globalized context.
