I am a researcher of film and media industries, currently preparing applications to PhD programs in Film and Media Studies. My M.A. thesis at Xiamen University asked why a distributor that releases films about grief and isolation became the coolest brand in American cinema — and what that success reveals about how independent film now works.

Working from Jerome Christensen's account of the corporate author, I treat A24 as an author with intentions and real control over its products, and place it inside the recent restructuring of the media industries. I then read its brand strategy through Bourdieu's sociology of taste and through the literature on cool, and compare A24 with Miramax and Neon in order to mark what has changed in independent film culture — and where A24's own crisis may lie.

My argument is that A24's success comes largely from its use of networked media and brand strategy in response to a youth culture that was already forming. In doing so it has helped produce a new cinephilia: one organized around coolness, loyalty to a company, and the affirmation of one's identity, rather than around the aesthetics of directors. What that shift does to the category of independent film itself is the question I want to keep pursuing.

My other work moves between Asian American cinema and cultural studies. A paper on Chinese American cinema reads the recurring mother–daughter conflict as a manifestation of the collective unconscious, then tests that pattern against three lenses — immigrant experience, commerce, and ideology. My undergraduate thesis used the Chinese critical concept of "chuzou" — departure, but also the power of women who resist — to think about female identity and cultural negotiation in Wayne Wang's films.
